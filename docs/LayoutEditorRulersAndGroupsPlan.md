# Layout Editor: Rulers, Default Categories, and Booth Groups

> Plan for four additions to the Layout editor on `feature/layout-editor`: a measured canvas (grid boxes, rulers, scale bar, cursor readout, zoom buttons), research on how layout editors handle editing, default booth categories so booths no longer start Unassigned, and booth groups by name and size. It builds on `LayoutEditorPlan.md` and follows `ArchitectureGuide.md` and `DeveloperAgreement.md`. Numbers about the mock data were measured on `annyeong-market-layout.json` and `beyond-market-layout.json`.

## Research: how layout editors handle editing

### What I could check

The session's network allowed searches but blocked most product sites, so the findings below come from search results and the documentation pages that loaded. Where a claim rests on my own knowledge of a product, it says so.

| Source | What it shows |
| ---| --- |
| EXPOCAD, as documented in Advanced Solutions' iMIS help ([change a booth number](https://documentation.advsol.com/imis/v2.0/docs/to-change-a-booth-number), [change the booth class](https://documentation.advsol.com/imis/v2.0/docs/to-change-the-booth-class), [interface overview](https://documentation.advsol.com/imis/v2.0/docs/overview-expocad-interface)) | Booths carry a number and a class. Both are changed by selecting the booth and using a menu command, and each change is logged as an activity. Floor plans can be generated by fitting booths of a default size ("Max Fit") |
| Map Your Show Show Builder (from its product page as summarized in search results; the page itself would not load) | Mass editing of booth types and properties, highlighting booths by type, and auditing booths against a reference layout for size, placement, and numbering |
| Cvent Event Diagramming ([product page](https://www.cvent.com/en/event-diagramming-and-seating), [Skift review](https://meetings.skift.com/cvent-social-tables-review)) | To-scale 2D editing built from venue drawings, with a 3D preview. Grid lines are optional on printouts. Third-party exam guides describe snap to grid, snap to object, and a ruler tool for measuring distances; I could not confirm those names in Cvent's own help |
| AppKit `NSButton.allowsMixedState` ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsbutton/allowsmixedstate)) | macOS controls have a third, mixed state for when a setting applies to only part of a selection |
| Figma, Sketch, Keynote (my own knowledge; Figma's help pages would not load here) | Rulers along the top and left that follow pan and zoom; a multi-selection shows "Mixed" in any field whose values differ, and typing into it sets every selected layer; Shift-click adds to or removes from a selection; Escape clears it |

### What that means for this app

| Pattern seen | Decision here |
| ---| --- |
| Booths are identified by a number with a letter prefix that groups them (EXPOCAD numbers, the mock's `A1` to `A112`) | The letter prefix defines a name group. Groups are derived from labels, not stored |
| Bulk edits are done on a set of booths of one kind (Map Your Show's mass editing, Figma's multi-selection) | Selecting a group opens a group inspector. One Save writes every member and is one undo step |
| Fields that differ across a selection read as mixed and only change when edited (Figma's "Mixed", AppKit's mixed state) | The group inspector shows "Mixed" for differing values and writes only the fields the user changed |
| Booths of one type can be highlighted together (Map Your Show) | Hovering a group in the list outlines its members on the canvas |
| To-scale editing needs visible scale: grid, rulers, measuring (Cvent, every design tool) | Rulers on two edges, grid boxes that adapt to zoom, a scale bar, a cursor readout in metres, and live dimensions on the selected object |
| Snap to grid is standard; snap to objects and alignment guides are common extras | Keep the existing 0.25 m snap. Alignment guides are listed under Out of scope for a later round |
| Destructive bulk actions confirm with a count (common macOS pattern, e.g. "Delete 3 Items") | Deleting a selected group asks "Delete 112 booths?" |

The mock data supports name groups well: every prefix holds one booth type, and every name group in both files except Annyeong's `C` has a single size (see section 3).

## 1. Measured canvas: grid, rulers, scale bar, readout, zoom

### What the reference shows

Grid boxes over the floor (about 1 m boxes at that zoom), rulers on the top and left labelled every 10 m ("0 m", "10 m", ... "80 m"), hatched closed areas, a "20 m" scale bar at the bottom right, "+", "−", and "fit" buttons at the top right, and a readout at the bottom left ("x 91.1 m, y 42.3 m · obstacle · 0.0 p/m²").

### What exists today

`FloorRenderer.drawGrid` draws 1 m minor and 5 m major lines in `canvasGridLine` (`#071327`) on the `#000C20` background. The lines sit under the floor polygons, so the white floor covers them: there are no grid boxes where booths are, which is where the user needs them. There are no rulers, scale bar, readout, or zoom buttons; zoom works through pinch, scroll, and the View menu.

### Spacing rules

One pure type, `RulerScale`, picks every step from the current scale (points per metre), so the grid, rulers, and scale bar always agree.

*   Nice steps: 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 m.
*   Ruler label step: the smallest nice step at least 64 points apart on screen. At the default fitted scale of about 10 points per metre, that is 10 m, which matches the reference.
*   Ruler minor ticks: label step divided by 5 (by 4 for a 2 m label step, by 2 for 0.5 m), shown only if at least 6 points apart.
*   Grid minor boxes: the smallest of 0.5, 1, 5, 10 m that is at least 8 points apart. Grid major lines: the smallest of 1, 5, 10, 50, 100 m that is at least 5 times the minor step (0.5 m boxes get 5 m lines, 1 m boxes get 5 m lines, 5 m boxes get 50 m lines).
*   Scale bar: the nice step whose length is closest to 120 points without going under 80 or over 200.

### Grid

*   Draw the grid in two passes: outside the floor in `canvasGridLine` as today, and over the floor, clipped to the hall and lobby polygons, in a new `canvasGridOnFloor` token (`canvasLine` at 10% for minor, 18% for major). Booths, tables, and other objects draw on top of the grid.
*   Grid lines align to whole metres from the layout origin, so a booth snapped to the 0.25 m grid lines up with the boxes.
*   Closed areas switch from a dashed outline with a pale fill to diagonal hatching (lines every 0.8 m at 45 degrees, `canvasLine` at 35%), clipped to the area, as in the reference.

### Rulers

*   A top band 24 points tall and a left band 36 points wide, drawn over the canvas in `surface` with a `surfaceBorder` edge. A 36 x 24 corner square shows "m".
*   Labels read "10 m" in `Typography.caption`, `textSecondary`. Ticks in `textTertiary`.
*   The rulers measure from the layout origin and move with pan and zoom. Negative values show with a minus sign.
*   While the pointer is over the canvas, a 1-point `accent` marker on each ruler shows its x and y.
*   While an object is selected, each ruler shades its extent (`accent` at 20%), so the user can read its position against the scale.
*   The canvas hit area starts below and right of the rulers, so clicks on a ruler never select an object.

### Scale bar

Bottom-right of the visible canvas, left of the inspector when one is shown: a horizontal bar of the chosen length with end caps and a label above ("20 m"), in `textSecondary` over a `surface` chip at 80% opacity.

### Cursor readout

Bottom-left, a chip in the same style: "x 31.4 m, y 42.3 m", followed by the object under the pointer when there is one: "· Booth A-12 · Fashion · 2.5 × 2.5 m". Doors read "· Door A · Entrance · 3.5 m". Empty floor reads "· Floor"; outside the floor reads "· Outside". The reference's crowd density ("0.0 p/m²") belongs to the simulation and is not shown here.

The readout and the ruler markers update on every pointer move. They must not do that through `LayoutEditorViewModel`, because the editor body reads the view model and would redraw on every move (the problem fixed in the last round). Use a small `@Observable` `CanvasPointer` object owned by `LayoutEditorView`, written by the canvas's hover handler and read only by the readout and the rulers.

### Zoom buttons

Three 36-point circular buttons ("+", "−", "fit" as in the reference) in `surface` with `surfaceBorder`, stacked vertically at the bottom-right above the scale bar. The Hi-Fi puts the inspector on the right, so the reference's top-right spot is taken; bottom-right keeps them next to the scale bar. They call the existing `zoomIn`, `zoomOut`, and `fitToView`. Accessibility labels "Zoom in", "Zoom out", "Zoom to fit".

### Live dimensions

*   The selected object shows its width along its top edge and its length along its left edge ("2.5 m"), in its own frame, so a rotated booth's labels rotate with it. Shown at 8 points per metre and above.
*   While dragging, a chip next to the pointer shows the offset ("Δ 1.25 m, −0.50 m").
*   A selected group shows its bounding size instead ("Group A · 13 rows" is drawn by the group overlay, see section 3).

## 2. Default categories

### Today

`CategoryMatcher.entry(for:in:)` maps `fnb` to the first legend entry whose name contains "food", "f&b", or "fnb", and maps everything else to nothing. Every `goods` booth starts Unassigned: 167 of 209 booths in Annyeong, 84 of 154 in Beyond.

### Rule

1.  `fnb` booths get the food entry, as today.
2.  `goods` booths are grouped by name prefix (section 3). The prefixes are sorted alphabetically, and each gets the next non-food legend entry in legend order, cycling.
3.  If the legend has no food entry, `fnb` booths stay Unassigned. If it has no non-food entries, `goods` booths stay Unassigned. An empty legend leaves everything Unassigned.
4.  The rule runs once, when a layout is mapped. After that the category is user data; changing the legend later only clears IDs that no longer exist (the existing `DraftLayoutMerger` step).

The cycle is a placeholder for what a real reader will detect from the legend colors on the floor plan. It is deterministic, puts every booth in a category, and keeps each name group in one category, so the user can fix a wrong guess for a whole group with one group edit.

### Result on the mock data (legend FNB, Fashion, Lifestyle & Hobbies)

| Layout | FNB | Fashion | Lifestyle & Hobbies | Unassigned |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Annyeong | F (42) | A (112), C (16) = 128 | B (30), I (9) = 39 | 0 |
| Beyond | F (27), G (25), H (18) = 70 | A (9), C (21), J (4), X (3) = 37 | B (26), D (8), K (10), XX (3) = 47 | 0 |

### Where it lives

`CategoryMatcher` gains `defaultCategories(for booths:in legend:) -> [String: LegendEntry.ID]`, keyed by booth label, using `BoothGrouping.namePrefix(of:)` for the prefix. `MockLayoutMapper.mapInitialBooths` looks each booth up in that map. The seeded Debug project gets the new defaults automatically because it goes through the same mapper.

## 3. Booth groups

### Definitions

All in a new pure type, `BoothGrouping`, in `Core/Domain/`.

| Term | Rule |
| ---| --- |
| Name prefix | The leading letters of the label, uppercased: `A12` gives `A`, `XX2` gives `XX`, `f3` gives `F`. A label with no leading letters (for example `12`) gives `#` |
| Name group | All booths with the same name prefix |
| Size key | Width and length, each rounded to the nearest 0.5 m, sorted short side first, so a rotated booth matches an unrotated one: 2.49 x 2.53 gives 2.5 x 2.5; 2.0 x 2.1 gives 2.0 x 2.0; 5.0 x 2.5 gives 2.5 x 5.0 |
| Size group | All booths with the same size key |
| Cluster | Inside one group, booths whose bounding boxes are less than 0.3 m apart on both axes are joined (transitively). A cluster is one row or block on the canvas |

Rounding to 0.5 m matters: at 0.1 m, tracing noise splits Annyeong into 11 size groups (2.0 x 2.0 and 2.0 x 2.1 apart, 2.5 x 2.5 and 2.5 x 2.6 apart); at 0.5 m it gives 4.

### Selecting a group

| Action | Result |
| ---| --- |
| Double-click a booth | Selects its name group |
| Option-double-click a booth | Selects its size group |
| "Select group A · 112" in the booth inspector | Selects the name group of the selected booth |
| "Select size 2.5 × 2.5 m · 150" in the booth inspector | Selects its size group |
| Click a row in the Groups list | Selects that group |
| Shift-click a booth | Adds it to or removes it from the selection. The result is a custom selection ("12 booths"), not a named group |
| Click a single booth without Shift | Back to a single selection |
| Click empty floor, or Escape | Clears the selection |

Groups are booths only, and only in the Floor Plan tab. Shift-click on a non-booth is ignored.

### Group inspector

Shown when two or more booths are selected.

*   Title: "Group A · 112 booths", "Size 2.5 × 2.5 m · 150 booths", or "12 booths" for a custom selection.
*   Category: the popup shows the shared category, or "Mixed" when members differ. Picking an entry sets it for every member.
*   Size: width and length fields show the shared value, or are empty with a "Mixed" placeholder. Typing a value sets that dimension for every member and keeps each booth's center.
*   Rotation: shared value or "Mixed"; picking one sets it for every member.
*   Open sides: not editable as a group, because they depend on each booth's neighbours. A button "Reset open sides for 112 booths" re-runs inference on every member.
*   A line under the title lists warnings for members, for example "3 booths overlap other objects".
*   Save Changes writes only the fields the user changed, as one undo step. The unsaved-changes dialog works as it does for one booth.
*   A "Delete 112 booths" button sits above Save, and the Delete key does the same; both ask for confirmation.

Resizing a group in place can make neighbours overlap; the validator shows that as it does for one booth, and nothing blocks the save.

### Groups list

When nothing is selected in the Floor Plan tab, the inspector panel shows a Groups list instead of being hidden:

*   Title "Booth Groups", two sections "By name" and "By size", sorted by prefix and by count.
*   Each row: a colored dot per category used by the group (up to three, then "+n"), the group title ("A" or "2.5 × 2.5 m"), and the count.
*   Hovering a row outlines its members on the canvas. Clicking selects the group.
*   The Flow tab keeps today's behavior (no panel when nothing is selected).

### Groups on the canvas

*   At 6 points per metre and above, each cluster of each name group gets a small chip at its top-left corner with the prefix ("A"), in `surface` with a dot of the group's category color. Annyeong shows 34 chips at most, Beyond 48.
*   A selected group draws every member with a 2-point `highlight` outline (members are not filled orange; that stays the single-selection look), and a dashed outline around each cluster's bounding box. The chip of each cluster reads "A · 112".
*   A hovered group, from the list, draws the same outlines at 50% opacity.

### What group selection does not do in this round

*   Arrow keys, R, and dragging act on single objects only. Dragging a member of a selected group selects just that booth and drags it.
*   Duplicating a group is not offered.

### Labels for new and duplicated booths

Today new and duplicated booths get `B` plus the next free number, so a new booth joins name group `B` by accident. Change it:

*   Duplicate keeps the source's prefix: duplicating `A5` gives `A113` in Annyeong.
*   Add Booth Here uses the prefix `N` ("new"), so added booths form their own group until renamed.

### Edits

`LayoutEdit` gains `case batch([LayoutEdit])`. Applying runs the edits in order; the inverse is the batch of inverses in reverse order. History records a batch as one entry. Group saves, group resets, and group deletes are batches. Group deletes are recorded in descending index order, so undo reinserts every booth at its original index.

## Design gaps and defaults

| Gap | Default |
| ---| --- |
| The Hi-Fi has no rulers, readout, or zoom buttons | Add them as described, in the dark palette |
| Zoom buttons at top-right in the reference collide with the inspector | Bottom-right, above the scale bar |
| Category colors are not shown on booths in the Hi-Fi | Still not filled; shown as dots on group chips, in the Groups list, and in the readout |
| Which non-food category each `goods` group gets | Alphabetical prefixes cycling through non-food entries; the user fixes whole groups |
| What a group of mixed sizes does with the size fields | Empty with a "Mixed" placeholder until typed into |
| Groups list replacing the empty inspector space | Floor Plan tab only |
| The reference shows density (p/m²) in the readout | Not shown; it belongs to the simulation |

## Out of scope

*   Moving or rotating a group as one unit.
*   Alignment guides and snap to objects.
*   A measuring tool between two points.
*   Renaming a group (changing every member's prefix).
*   Light canvas theme.
